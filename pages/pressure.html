PRESSURE • CHOICE • CONFIDENCE

You don't have to follow the crowd.

Peer pressure can make it difficult to say no. Understanding how to respond can help you protect your choices and your future.

YOUR CHOICE YOUR FUTURE
UNDERSTAND THE PRESSURE

Pressure doesn't always look obvious.

It can appear as a joke, a challenge, a repeated invitation or the feeling that everyone else is participating.

01
SOCIAL PRESSURE

“Come on, everyone is doing it.”

When a group makes something seem normal or expected, it can become harder to make your own decision.

02
DIRECT PRESSURE

“Just try it once.”

A direct invitation can make you feel that refusing will make you look different or unpopular.

03
EMOTIONAL PRESSURE

“If you're my friend, you'll do it.”

Real friendship should not depend on someone ignoring their boundaries or doing something they are uncomfortable with.

PRACTICAL RESPONSES

Four ways to handle peer pressure.

01

Say No

Keep your response simple and confident. You don't owe anyone a long explanation.

02

Change the Situation

Suggest another activity or move away from the situation if you feel uncomfortable.

03

Stay With Supportive People

Being around people who respect your decisions can make it easier to maintain your boundaries.

04

Ask for Help

If pressure becomes difficult to manage, talk to someone you trust.

REAL-LIFE SCENARIO
COLLEGE • SOCIAL SITUATION 01

Your friends offer you a substance and say:

“Relax. Don't be boring. Just try it once.”

What would be the safest response?

REMEMBER

Saying no doesn't make you less confident.

Confidence means being able to make a decision that is right for you—even when others disagree.
